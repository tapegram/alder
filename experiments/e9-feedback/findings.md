# E9 findings: survivors as feedback

*Run 2026-10-12, branch `e9-feedback`.* Three questions:

- **H1, the score:** how many of `alder test --mutate`'s mutants do the tests Claude Haiku, Sonnet and Opus write
  kill, by task, and which kinds survive?
- **H2, feedback:** after the first round that checks, builds and passes its own tests, the surviving mutants are fed
  back (up to two rounds, app code frozen). Does the score rise, and are the new tests real?
- **H3, behaviour:** do E8's hidden acceptance tests still pass?

Matrix: Haiku and Sonnet × {`baseline`, `feedback`} × T5, T6, T7, T9, T10 × 2 samples, Opus × both × the five × 1;
50 cells. All run with `--build --test --mutate --retries 2 --hidden`; `feedback` adds `--feedback 2`.

Where the data is:

- raw data: `{baseline,feedback}/runs/<model>/<task>-<sample>/`;
- the table: `scores.md` (generated by `logs/scores.sh`);
- survivors, fed-back tests and equivalence claims: `survivors.md`;
- per-operator kill rates: `logs/operators.md`;
- the run log: `RUN.md`.

Every number below comes from the summaries, the round files or `logs/attribution.jsonl`, through the scripts in
`logs/` (`extract.py`, `attribute.py`, `operators.py`, `survivors.py`), unless it is marked *hand-scored*. A cell is
named `<cond>/<model>/<task>-<sample>`.

**The harness** (Task 3).

- M9's `--mutate` records the passing round's score and its survivors (file, line, kind, before → after).
- `--feedback N` adds up to N rounds after the first pass with survivors. Each prompt is the ruled text ("Your tests
  pass, but S of N mutants survived …") and at most 10 survivor lines.
- **The frozen-app rule:** a feedback round whose IR, minus `test`/`fake`/`module` forms, differs from the passing
  round's is rejected.
- A feedback round that fails gets one retry, within N.
- The hidden tests run once, on the last accepted round.

**Binary.** Clean `git archive` export of `5689a70`, sha256 `97b9f629…c3cb0e`; prompt, sizes and scripts in `5b46f6c`.

### The fed-back tests are real *(hand-scored, `survivors.md` §2)*

The 39 accepted rounds added 355 tests: 350 *real*, 5 *wrong-module*, **0 tautologies**. The tautology risk
(Review Focus 1) did not occur. The real ones break down as follows:

- **298 kill a survivor of their own module** when run alone.
- **36 kill only mutants that other tests already kill.** These restate an existing check, often as a browser
  version of an `update` test.
- **16 kill no mutant.** Each was read by hand. Most check behaviour that no operator changes: six unknown-id tests
  (`PageMissing` or `Err "not found"`), pure-function boundary cases, markup. Two are positive-only checks that a
  dropped condition still satisfies: `feedback/claude-haiku/t9-bookings-s1`'s two `Maybe.isJust (overlapping …)`

The 5 *wrong-module* tests kill the survivor they answer, but from a page module rather than the declaring module
the prompt named:

- `App`'s logout redirect, tested from `Posts.Index` (`feedback/claude-sonnet/t7-drafts-s1`,
  `feedback/claude-opus/t7-drafts-s1`);
- `Member.add` (`feedback/claude-haiku/t10-teams-s2`, two tests);
- `Product`'s default (`feedback/claude-sonnet/t6-orders-s1`).

### Equivalence claims *(hand-scored, `survivors.md` §5)*

20 comments say that a survivor cannot be told apart:

- **9 are correct.** These are the blog's page-clamp and limit equivalents, and T9's boundary flips, where both
  branches give the same date.
- **6 are plausible.** Each is a `where` that only narrows a read the code filters again, or a default that a
  reference rule makes unreachable.
- **5 are wrong.**
  - Four are the login arms, each citing the Password sentence. Haiku's and Sonnet's were contradicted by their own

    r3).
  - The fifth, in a failed round, says `Reject` cannot be told from `Rerender`. A test of `update` sees the `Nav`
    value, so it can.

### The frozen-app rule rejected one round of 45

The rejected round is `feedback/claude-haiku/t7-drafts-s2` r2. It was a refactor for testability that kept behaviour:
it extracted `signIn` from `Auth.Login`'s `Submit` arm and added 6 tests. The rule worked as written, and the cell lost
its second round: it ended at 0.82, the lowest feedback score apart from the failed cells.

No round removed or weakened app code to drop a mutant (Review Focus 2). All 39 accepted rounds kept the first pass's
app hash.

### Failed feedback rounds

5 of the 45 rounds failed, in 3 cells. The retry fired 3 times and passed once (`survivors.md` §3):

- `feedback/claude-haiku/t10-teams-s1` r1 failed with E0003; its retry was accepted.
- `feedback/claude-haiku/t6-orders-s2` r2 failed with E0001×2, then its retry with E0003×2 (a record not annotated as
  `Model`).
- `feedback/claude-sonnet/t6-orders-s2` r1 failed with E0001×2. Its retry then failed 1 of its own 40 tests: the new
  test expected 1 form where the page draws 3.

Both E0001 failures are the same construct: a test applies a pure function to list literals that start on the next
lines.

```alder
test "total sums quantity times price over the lines" =
  total
    [ … ]
```

Book 3 says an expression continues onto the next line only when the line ends with an operator, when the next line
begins with `|>` or an operator, or inside unclosed brackets. So the parser ends the application at `total`. Two of
the 25 cells (8%) ended on this rule and kept their first-pass scores (0.42 and 0.31).

### Power

Each model and task has at most 2 samples (1 for Opus). The direction is not in doubt: 23 of 25 cells rose, by
0.16–0.70, and the two that did not are failures of the round, not of the feedback. The data does not separate the
models' gains: Opus starts higher and ends at 0.96 against 0.90 and 0.91, a difference of two or three cells.

## 4. H3: hidden acceptance tests

**386 / 390.** All four failures are one test, "the owner removes a member, who then loses the team", in all four
Haiku T10 cells (`baseline` s1 and s2, `feedback` s1 and s2):

```text
left:  { answer: Rerendered, members: ["Ann", "Cid"], after: PageMissing, teams: 0 }
right: { answer: Redirected /teams/…, members: ["Ann", "Cid"], after: PageMissing, teams: 0 }
```

The member is removed and loses the team; only the answer differs. The Names block says that Remove and SendInvite
"Both answer `Redirect (Teams.Show { id: … })`", but Haiku's `Remove` answers `Rerender` (class *redirect*,
*hand-scored*).

Mutation testing cannot see this:

- There is no `Redirect` there to mutate.
- Haiku's own test asserts the `Rerendered` it wrote (`feedback/claude-haiku/t10-teams-s2` r3, `Teams.Show`).

A test that agrees with a wrong program kills its mutants just as well, so the hidden tests remain the only check of
the program against the task.

**Feedback could not change a hidden result** (Review Focus 5). The hidden tests ran once per cell, on `hidden_round`,
the last accepted round. Every accepted round's app IR hash equals its first pass's (39 / 39).

**The relaxed refusal tests held.**

- **T6:** the relaxed stale-page test passed 10 / 10; E8's strict version had failed every `without` cell. The server
  answered `Rerendered` (a `Reject`) in 8 cells and `RequestFailed` in 2 (`baseline/claude-haiku/t6-orders-s2`,
  `feedback/claude-haiku/t6-orders-s1`). Under E8's test, 8 of the 10 would have failed.
- **T9:** the Cancel of a started booking answered `Rerendered` in 10 / 10.
- **T10:** the owner's own Remove answered `PageMissing` in 8 cells and `Rerendered` in 2.

Nothing changed in any of them.

## 7. What changes because of this

### The task texts: the E8 lesson about hints, again

"Do not write tests that call the `Password` capability" was meant to keep tests off the real Go adapter. The models
read it as a ban on login tests:

- all 50 first passes left all three login mutants;
- 4 of the 5 wrong equivalence claims cite the sentence;
- the only rejected round was a workaround for it.

The text should say what is wanted, for example "log in through the form with `Test.fake Password` (chapter 10);
never the real adapter". E8 found the same thing from the other side: a sentence that names a mechanism decides what
the model does.

The Haiku T10 redirect (§4) suggests a second fix: one message, one redirect line. "Both answer …", placed after the
second message, was misread in 4 of 4 Haiku cells and in no Sonnet or Opus cell.

### The reference and the book

Book 10 already has "A surviving mutant is a test you have not written". These models never ran `alder` themselves:
the harness gives them no tools. So the data cannot say whether a louder sentence would help. It does say what the loop
does: one feedback round took Haiku and Sonnet from about 0.5 to 0.9, with real tests.

The book's examples could add the tests every first pass lacked:

- a login through the form with the fake;
- a visitor redirected from each `requires user` page;
- a delete refused by `@restrict`;
- the order of a list.

Their operators account for 221 of the 475 `baseline` survivors: `cascade ↔ restrict` 55, `requires dropped` 53, the
login arms 75, `orderBy reversed` 38.

Chapter 3's continuation rule needs an example of the failing shape, a function applied to arguments on the next
lines. It cost 2 of the 25 feedback cells.

### The language and the tool

- **Operators.** Every operator was killed somewhere except `limit dropped` (0 / 5), and all five of those are the
  blog's equivalent.
- **Two recurring equivalent patterns.** `--mutate` already suppresses some equivalents; these two could be added:
  - the boundary flip in `if a < b then b else a` and its `>`/`>=` forms, where both branches agree at equality (T9,
    4 cells);
  - the blog's page clamp and its over-fetch limit (T7, 5 cells).

  Together they are 19 of the 45 survivors left in the 23 completed feedback cells. Suppressing them would make 1.00
  mean "every test written".
- **The continuation rule.** The parser could accept an indented argument line, or E0001 could name the rule. Either
  is a language decision; the data only shows the cost.
- **The frozen-app rule** is right as a measurement rule. A loop for real use might allow a refactor that keeps
  behaviour, such as the rejected `signIn` extraction. E9 cannot tell such a refactor from a weakening.

### The next experiment

- **The text fix.** The cheapest test: re-run the `baseline` condition with the Password sentence rewritten, and watch
  the 75 / 75 login survivors.
- **Feedback in the default loop.** The larger question is whether survivor feedback belongs in the default agent
  loop. `--feedback 1` gives most of the gain. Answering it needs a cost measure the harness lacks today: Claude token
  counts.
- **More samples** would separate the models' gains. The direction needs no more.

### Roadmap candidates

- **Generators and admin.** A generator that emits a page with its `requires`, its delete rules and the login form
  could emit the matching tests too. These are exactly the survivors every model left (§2).
- **Jobs and distribution.** E9 has no data on them.

## What this run does not show

- **Whether a book change would raise the unprompted score.** The book was the same in both conditions; only the loop
  differed.
